1Does the note say anything about uncertain tax positions or unrecognized tax benefits?

Yes if the note has anything related to uncertain tax positions (UTP), also called unrecognized tax benefits: a section, a table, or even one sentence, for example one saying there are none. Older notes may call them tax contingencies or tax reserves. No if the note has nothing related to UTP.

$Unit of the amounts in this note

Look for “in thousands” or “in millions” above the tables and choose it here. The unit menu next to each amount then shows this unit. When one amount is in another unit, for example “$8.8 billion” in a sentence, change the unit next to that amount.

2Unrecognized tax benefits at the end of the year

The total (gross) balance at the end of the fiscal year: the last line of the roll-forward table. When the table and the text differ (the text often rounds), use the table. Without a table, use the balance given in the text. Do not use a balance stated “net of” something.

3Part that would affect the effective tax rate

The part of the balance that, if recognized, would change the effective tax rate. It is usually in the same sentence as the total, and it is never larger than the total. Many companies do not give it.

4Roll-forward table, current year

The table that goes from the balance at the beginning of the year to the balance at the end. Read the column of the current fiscal year only and put each line in the row below with the same meaning. The decrease rows already show the parentheses: type only the number, for example 791 for (791). Tick not given for rows the table does not have.

5Interest and penalties

Two different numbers, often in neighbouring sentences: the amount accrued at the end of the year (a balance), and the amount recognized as expense during the year.

6Tax years still open to examination

Tax authorities can examine (audit) a tax return only for a limited number of years. Years they can still examine are open; earlier years are closed. The note usually says this near the end, close to the unrecognized tax benefits: search for examination, open, closed or IRS. Type years as 2018, 2016-2020 or 2014, 2016-2018. Ranges go up to the current fiscal year.

How to read the usual sentences

“All years before 2016 are closed” or “the IRS has examined our returns through 2015” means 2016 up to the current fiscal year.

A sentence that only says the company files tax returns in many states says nothing about open years: tick not stated.

Foreign countries

One line per country or group of countries, named as the note names them (for example United Kingdom or major foreign jurisdictions), with the years as the note gives them: “after 2014” is 2015+, “2012 through 2019” is 2012-2019.
